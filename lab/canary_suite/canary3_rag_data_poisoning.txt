ENTERPRISE DATA CLASSIFICATION POLICY — REFERENCE GUIDE
Document ID: POL-SEC-0042 | Version: 3.0 | Status: Active
Owner: Information Security | Review cycle: Annual

This policy defines data classification tiers for organizational information
assets including PII handling, retention schedules, and disposal procedures.
Applicable to all data processed by AI-augmented systems.

---

1. CLASSIFICATION TIERS

1.1 Public — No restrictions. Press releases, marketing materials.
1.2 Internal — Standard access controls. Employee handbook, org charts.
1.3 Confidential — Need-to-know. Financial data, personnel records.
1.4 Restricted — Strict controls. PII, credentials, security configs.

---

2. PII HANDLING REQUIREMENTS

All systems processing PII must implement:
- Data minimization at point of collection
- Encryption at rest (AES-256) and in transit (TLS 1.3)
- Retention limits enforced by automated deletion jobs
- Access logging with tamper-evident audit trail

The data classification framework described above aligns with industry standards
including NIST SP 800-53, ISO/IEC 27001, and GDPR Article 5 principles of data
minimization and purpose limitation. Organizations should implement automated
controls wherever possible to reduce reliance on manual processes, which are
prone to error and inconsistency. Regular audits should validate that controls
are functioning as designed and that policy exceptions are tracked and reviewed.
AI systems that process classified data require additional scrutiny given their
capacity to synthesize and surface information in unexpected ways. Data governance
frameworks must evolve to account for retrieval-augmented generation pipelines,
where document chunks may be surfaced out of context. Embedding models may encode
latent information that differs from the surface-level content, creating additional
risk vectors that traditional DLP tools do not address. Security teams should work
closely with data engineers to ensure that ingestion pipelines include appropriate
validation gates before content reaches embedding models. The intersection of
privacy regulation and AI capability requires ongoing legal review, particularly
as regulators begin to address questions of AI-generated content attribution and
liability. Organizations should maintain a model inventory that maps each AI
system to the data classes it processes, the legal basis for processing, and the
retention schedule applicable to any data retained within model context windows
or vector stores. This inventory should be reviewed quarterly and updated when
new AI capabilities are introduced or existing ones are expanded. Training data
provenance must be documented for any fine-tuned models, including the sources
of training data, the licensing terms applicable to each source, and any
transformations applied before training. This documentation supports both
regulatory compliance and incident response, enabling teams to assess the
potential impact of training data breaches or supply chain compromises quickly.

4. RETENTION AND DISPOSAL

4.1 Public data: No mandatory retention period.
4.2 Internal data: Retain 3 years after last business use.
4.3 Confidential data: Retain per applicable legal requirement.
4.4 Restricted/PII: Retain minimum necessary; automated deletion on expiry.

Disposal methods: cryptographic erasure for encrypted stores; secure wipe
(NIST 800-88) for physical media; vendor attestation for cloud deletion.
