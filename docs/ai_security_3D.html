The three dimensions

An AI system is evaluated by deployment pattern (what is being built or procured), technical surface (where threats can manifest), and control evidence (what must be implemented, tested, monitored, and evidenced).

Each team has historically anchored on one axis. AppSec reasoned about patterns, Engineering reasoned about surfaces, GRC reasoned about controls. None of those views is wrong. The matrix below makes the intersections explicit so the three views compose into one.

Governance, identity/tenancy, supply chain, and testing/monitoring are cross-cutting control planes that apply to every pattern and surface. They are filterable (chips below) rather than columns of their own, because they cut across every cell rather than living in any one.

Click any cell to expand pattern-specific threats, countermeasures, NIST SP 800-53 v5 IDs (PRIMARY), ISO/IEC 42001 Annex A anchors (ACCENT), AI-XXX family tags (INK), and operational evidence fields.

Intensity scoring criteria

Fig. 02 — Definitions
Critical

Surface can directly cause material confidentiality, integrity, availability, tenant-isolation, regulatory, financial, or customer-impact failure for that pattern. Primary defensive focus.

High

Significant surface where attack chains commonly land. Controls required; gaps create non-trivial residual risk even if not directly catastrophic.

Medium

Present surface needing baseline controls. Either secondary in attack chains or substantially mitigated by an upstream control that should still be verified.

Minimal

Baseline hygiene only. Surface exists but is either provider-controlled, structurally constrained, or below normal threat-modelling priority for this pattern.

Intensity is rated from the organization's defensive-surface perspective — how much control work the pattern requires from the defender to mitigate the surface. Inherent risk before controls may be higher. This convention should be confirmed at first joint working session and made consistent across all cells.

Cross-cutting control planes

Fig. 04 — Filterable, not stacked
Plane Definition Why it cuts across, not stacks
C0 · OVERLAYGovernance, Risk & Evidence AI inventory, ownership, risk tiering, approval, exceptions, evidence, periodic review, TPRM, regulatory mapping. Applies before design, during build, at release, in operation, and at retirement. Not a stage; a wrapper.
C1 · OVERLAYIdentity, Tenancy & Authorization Actor identity, effective identity, tenant context, delegated authorization, NHI lifecycle, tenant isolation, auditability. Must be enforced at data retrieval, prompt assembly, model call, tool execution, output disclosure, logs, and runtime. Not a column; a discipline.
C2 · OVERLAYSupply Chain & Provenance Datasets, models, registries, OSS components, MCP servers, SaaS providers, containers, CI/CD, infrastructure dependencies. Every surface has external dependencies. Provenance is a property of every artifact crossing a trust boundary.
C3 · OVERLAYTesting, Monitoring, IR & Recovery Pre-release security testing, red teaming, adversarial testing, regression, release gates, runtime observability, incident response, rollback. Lives before release and continues at runtime for every pattern/surface intersection. No surface is exempt.
